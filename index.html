The problem

Cloud AI is a liability in a regulated practice.

General-purpose AI tools are built for the open internet. In a clinic or a law firm, every prompt you type can carry protected health information or privileged client material — and it lands on someone else's server.

Risk 01

The data leaves the building

A cloud assistant sends your documents off-site the moment you use it. In a clinic that's PHI; in a firm it's privileged client material sitting in a stranger's logs.

Risk 02

Compliance becomes your problem

HIPAA expects safeguards and a signed business associate agreement around PHI. Privilege and the duty of confidentiality don't survive a copy that lives on a vendor's infrastructure.

Risk 03

Costs scale with every click

Per-seat, per-token cloud pricing grows as your team adopts the tool — and it's impossible to forecast. A local model is a fixed cost you already own.

Risk 04

No internet, no assistant

Cloud tools stop the second your connection does. Exam rooms, trial prep, and closing days don't get to wait for a reliable uplink.

Questions

The things practices and firms ask first.

Does this need an internet connection?

No. The assistant runs on your local network, so it keeps working when your connection doesn't. Internet is only needed for occasional updates, which we schedule.

What hardware do we need?

Usually a single modern workstation on your network — nothing exotic and no new servers required to start. We spec the machine for your workload and budget during the assessment.

Is it HIPAA compliant?

We design to the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rule expectations around PHI — access control, audit logging, and keeping data on-premise. You remain the covered entity in control of your data, and we help with the risk assessment and paperwork that goes with that. We're not a law firm, and we don't hand out compliance guarantees.

Is it safe for privileged client material?

The design point is that your files never leave the firm's network. That removes the third-party exposure that makes cloud tools hard to square with the duty of confidentiality and your obligations under the rules of professional conduct.

Will this replace my staff?

No. It drafts, summarizes, and searches so your people spend time on judgment instead of typing. A human reviews everything that leaves the building.

Can it read the files we already have?

Yes — we point it at the folders and systems you choose, on your own machines. Nothing is uploaded anywhere, and you can revoke access at any time.
